# Editorial rules

These rules adapt the compositional method of *What Takes Hold* to *Russian Ark*, incorporating the author's subsequent instructions.

- Choose the scene, action and intended boundaries before searching for borrowed language. Read each candidate in its original context, then test it in the complete chapter. Keep language that improves voice, character, humour, imagery or practical specificity. Reject language that makes the scene generic, introduces an unwanted person or event, or misstates the action.
- Build the literary version entirely from source words. Juxtapose and trim fragments from different works to make new sentences and paragraphs. Each fragment must be contiguous in its source and separately traceable. Omitted material creates a new fragment boundary; it does not license rewriting the remaining words.
- Allow quotation-boundary changes to capitalization and punctuation, as in *What Takes Hold*. Preserve the lexical wording and interior punctuation of each selected span. Add no original connecting words. Use punctuation to express the actual syntax and rhythm of the new sentence.
- Normally prefer excerpts of at least four words. Apply that guideline in proportion to the sentence: a three-word excerpt can suit a four-word target, and four quoted words can suit a five- or seven-word target. Retain essential shorter replies, names, gestures or subjects when longer wording would distort the scene. The author expressly permits a two-word excerpt such as “the Germans” when needed for the scene. Do not pad a sentence merely to meet a word count.
- Match each scene's implied century, using the original work's publication date or a stated composition date. Record translation dates separately. Contemporary and undated scenes use twentieth-century literature. Prefer Russian literature where it fits.
- Keep the 29 dated chapters in order. Dates belong in both versions' chapter headings. The complete sourced version comes first, all references follow it, and the complete prose version comes last. Preserve the prose version and historical dates during revisions to the cento.
- Number distinct source passages consecutively by first appearance across the whole literary version. An exact repeat from the same work and location reuses its number; a different span receives a different number. Boundary capitalization or punctuation changes alone do not create a new passage identity. Retain bracketed linked citations, italic titles and hanging reference indents.
- Verify wording, original context, century, printed location and scene fit. Check reference numbering and destinations after recomposition. Render the Word document and inspect the pages before delivery or publication.
